## 4 Results

Section [4.1](#sec-results-reconciliation) asks whether heterogeneous copper statistics can be reconciled into one consistent PSUT, whether the adjustment this takes is compatible with their stated uncertainty, and where and how much copper moves (Figure [4](#fig-reconciliation), Table [3](#tab-ledger) rows A1–A5). Section [4.2](#sec-results-system) asks what the reconciled, uncertainty-weighted account shows that the source statistics cannot show separately (Figure [5](#fig-copper-system), rows B1–B3). Table 3 fixes each expectation, metric and threshold before the numbers were read (SI S15).

<a id="sec-results-reconciliation"></a>

### 4.1 Reconciliation of the copper account

The reference run reconciles 28,482 edges per year against 1,952,021 constraint rows over 30 years; 28 years return *Solved* and two *AlmostSolved* at the numerical floor. Consistency is exact: the largest hard mass-balance residual in any year is $1.7 \times 10^{-7}$ kt (A1), and no flow other than the three signed stock changes is negative.

Compatibility with the stated uncertainty is a property of the row family, not of the account (Figure 4a). Over the period the objective splits into 54% edge defects, 44% scale-total residuals and 2% soft-ratio residuals, but the families differ by an order of magnitude in how often a row is moved beyond its own $3\sigma$: 0.4–2.4% of edges in any year against 19–32% of scale totals, rising after 2016. The sources disagree at the level of reported totals, which ICSG, BACI and the ore allocation cannot all be right about, while individual flows mostly stay within their wider stated uncertainty. A2 fails its pre-registered form only because three years fall to 0.19–0.20 on the scale-total clause.

The edge adjustment is localised on trade (Figure 4b, d). Two sector keys carry 85% of the edge objective, cathode to final use (53%) and anode to electrolytic refining (32%); 92% of it sits on cross-region legs and 71% on legs into the 13 regions that report no smelter, refinery or SX-EW production to ICSG (A3) — Switzerland, the Netherlands, Singapore, Luxembourg and BACI's other trading hubs, whose recorded imports the account cannot absorb. Among scale totals the residual concentrates on cathode (22%) and concentrate (15%) nodes; the ranked identities are in SI S16.

The copper that moves is small downstream and larger upstream (Figure 4c). Over 30 years the account retains 0.994 of the prior refined output, 0.992 of mine output in contained copper and 0.943 of concentrate intake, but only 0.862 of the crude-ore mass; scrap inputs are retained slightly above unity (1.015 into smelters). Chile's concentrate exports retain 0.979 over 2010–2019. A4 fails: the ore-mass step holds in 26 of 30 years, the cathode–concentrate step in six, because both copper stages now sit within 0.01 of the prior.

Termination accuracy is a first-order modelling choice (A5). Re-solving the base constraint set at the tightened tolerance, with nothing else changed, moves ore-mass retention from 0.779 to 0.861, concentrate output from 0.873 to 0.930, scrap into smelters from 0.995 to 0.772 and Chile's export retention from 0.574 to 0.893; five of nine stages move by more than 0.02. A retention figure from a $\sigma$-weighted reconciliation needs its termination rule beside it. No posterior $\sigma$ is reported, because Stage 5 has not run on the copper chain (issue #27); uncertainty enters these results only through $\sigma$ in $z$.

### 4.2 The copper system in one consistent account

The three insights below are read from one account, so each quantity respects every balance of Figure 2 in every region and year and can be set beside its prior.

**Secondary copper.** On the input side of refining — scrap into smelters and refineries over scrap, concentrate intake and SX-EW cathode — the world posterior share is 0.181 pooled over 2020–2024, within 0.02 of the 0.165 implied by the ICSG feed and beside ICSG's published 0.178 for 2024 (B1; the benchmark is an input, so this is fidelity, not validation). The regional picture the world number hides is stable across prior and posterior (Figure 5a): the EU-14 refine at 0.364, twice the world share; Japan, Korea and Taiwan at 0.256; China at 0.266 and rising since 2010, lifted by 0.04 against its prior, the largest such move; the United States at 0.046, because direct melt lies outside the boundary; India at 0.017; Chile and Peru at zero. The secondary share is a regional property of refining, not a global rate.

**Concentration along the chain.** The Herfindahl–Hirschman index of producer regions is drawn as a band whose width is the treatment of the rest-of-world aggregate (Figure 5b); it never exceeds 0.007. In 1995 every step was unconcentrated (0.08–0.12). By 2024 mining is still 0.103, with Chile at 0.21 of world output, while smelting stands at 0.274, refining at 0.221 and final use at 0.342, with China at 0.51, 0.44 and 0.57 of the respective totals. Concentration rises downstream of the mine and crosses the 0.25 line for smelting and use, but not for refining; B2 fails on that clause and on invariance, because the reconciliation moves the use-side index by 0.032 — it lifts China's cathode intake against its prior — and the other steps by less than 0.013.

**Trade relations.** Chile's exports of concentrate and cathode went from 2,126 kt CC in 1995, a third to Japan, Korea and Taiwan and a quarter to the EU-14, to 5,112 kt CC in 2024, 0.528 of them to China; Peru's from 403 to 2,803 kt CC, 0.699 to China; China's imports of concentrate, anode and cathode from 375 to 11,365 kt CC, 0.237 from Chile, 0.172 from Peru and 0.166 from the D.R. Congo (Figure 5c). The embodied view of Figure 5d is what a consistent PSUT adds. Propagating extraction through the process network, 15,256 kt CC of the copper in 2024's final use, 54% of world extraction, was consumed in China: 1,621 kt from Chinese mines, 3,128 from Chile, 2,684 from the D.R. Congo and 2,406 from Peru. Chile and Peru together supply 5,534 kt CC of China's consumption, 1.41 times the 3,932 kt CC they ship to China directly as concentrate; the rest arrives as cathode refined elsewhere or as anode through third countries (B3). Chile's share of exports going to China rose from 0.035 to 0.528.
