## 4 Results

Section [4.1](#sec-results-reconciliation) asks whether heterogeneous copper statistics can be reconciled into one consistent PSUT, whether the adjustment this takes is compatible with their stated uncertainty, and where and how much copper moves (Figure [4](#fig-reconciliation), Table [3](#tab-ledger) rows A1–A5). Section [4.2](#sec-results-system) asks what the reconciled, uncertainty-weighted account shows that the source statistics cannot show separately (Figure [5](#fig-copper-system), rows B1–B3).

<a id="sec-results-reconciliation"></a>

### 4.1 Reconciliation of the copper account

The reference run reconciles 28,482 edges per year against 1,687,240 constraint rows over 30 years; all 30 years return *Solved*. Consistency holds to the solver's floor: the largest hard mass-balance residual is $1.1 \times 10^{-6}$ kt, just above A1's threshold (Section 3.1.6), and no flow other than the three signed stock changes is negative.

Compatibility with the stated uncertainty is a property of the row family, not of the account (Figure 4a). Over the period the objective splits into 70% edge defects, 29% scale-total residuals and 0.2% soft-ratio residuals, but the families differ by an order of magnitude in how often a row is moved beyond its own $3\sigma$: 0.4–1.2% of edges in any year against 12–23% of scale totals, higher from 2017 (0.21 on average, against 0.17 before). The sources disagree at the level of reported totals, which ICSG, BACI and the ore allocation cannot all be right about, while individual flows mostly stay within their wider stated uncertainty. A2 fails its pre-registered form on the scale-total clause: in 21 years, all before 2019, the share stays at 0.12–0.20.

The edge adjustment is localised on trade (Figure 4b, d). Two sector keys carry 90% of the edge objective, cathode to final use (56%) and anode to electrolytic refining (34%); 95% of it sits on cross-region legs and 79% on legs into the 13 regions that report no smelter, refinery or SX-EW production to ICSG (A3) — Switzerland, the Netherlands, Singapore, Luxembourg and BACI's other trading hubs, whose recorded imports the account cannot absorb. Among scale totals the residual concentrates on cathode (27%) and concentrate (17%) nodes; the ranked identities are in SI S16.

Little copper moves at any stage (Figure 4c): over 30 years the account retains 0.997 of the prior refined output, 1.010 of mine output in contained copper, 0.964 of concentrate intake and 0.999 of the crude-ore mass; scrap is retained at 1.011 into smelters and 1.026 into refineries. Chile's concentrate exports retain 1.025 over 2010–2019. A4 fails in every year: the concentrate–ore step holds in ten, the cathode–concentrate step in one, because all three stages stay close to their priors (within 0.04 over the period). The upstream deletion it was written for (0.864 of the crude-ore mass on the previous reference chain) was where the solver stopped; solved for the defects, the same problem retains 0.998 (issue #33).

Termination accuracy is a first-order modelling choice (A5). Re-solving the base constraint set at the tightened tolerance, with nothing else changed, moves ore-mass retention from 0.779 to 0.861, scrap into smelters from 0.995 to 0.772 and Chile's export retention from 0.574 to 0.893; five of nine stages move by more than 0.02. A retention figure from a $\sigma$-weighted reconciliation needs its termination rule beside it. Under the stated $\sigma$, Stage 5 contracts the large flows strongly: the mass-weighted contraction of the feed-observed contained-copper edges is 0.93, so their posterior $\sigma$ is set by the constraints rather than by their own feed, while the median such edge contracts by only 0.065. The model's own residuals say its stated $\sigma$ are too small (Birge ratio 5.18; 13.1% of those edges beyond 3 standardised units against 0.27% expected), so intervals in this paper are Birge-scaled and remain lower bounds in the tail (Section 3.1.7, issue #87).

### 4.2 The copper system in one consistent account

The three insights below are read from one account, so each quantity respects every balance of Figure 2 in every region and year and can be set beside its prior.

**Secondary copper.** On the input side of refining — scrap into smelters and refineries over scrap, concentrate intake and SX-EW cathode — the world posterior share is 0.172 pooled over 2020–2024, within 0.02 of the 0.164 implied by the ICSG feed and beside ICSG's published 0.176 for 2024 (B1; the benchmark is an input, so this is fidelity, not validation). The regional picture the world number hides is stable across prior and posterior (Figure 5a): the EU-14 refine at 0.370, twice the world share; Japan, Korea and Taiwan at 0.249 and China at 0.240, each lifted by 0.013–0.014 against its prior; the United States at 0.045, because direct melt lies outside the boundary; India at 0.005; Chile and Peru at zero. The secondary share is a regional property of refining, not a global rate.

**Concentration along the chain.** The Herfindahl–Hirschman index of producer regions is drawn as a band for the treatment of the rest-of-world aggregate, at most 0.004 wide in 1995 and 2024 (Figure 5b). In 1995 every step was unconcentrated (0.08–0.12). By 2024 mining is still 0.111, with Chile at 0.24 of world output, while smelting stands at 0.281, refining at 0.230 and final use at 0.352, with China at 0.52, 0.45 and 0.58 of the respective totals. Concentration rises downstream of the mine and crosses the 0.25 line for smelting and use, but not for refining, whose whole interval lies below it (0.226–0.234); B2 fails on that clause alone, since the reconciliation moves no step's index by more than 0.003.

**Trade relations.** Chile's exports of concentrate and cathode went from 2,228 kt CC in 1995, a third to Japan, Korea and Taiwan and a quarter to the EU-14, to 5,507 kt CC in 2024, 0.548 of them to China (0.034 in 1995); Peru's from 396 to 2,787 kt CC, 0.710 to China; China's imports of concentrate, anode and cathode from 385 to 11,885 kt CC (Figure 5c). The embodied view of Figure 5d is what a consistent PSUT adds. Propagating extraction through the process network, 16,727 kt CC of the copper in 2024's final use, 56% of world extraction, was consumed in China, 3,886 kt of it mined in Chile and 2,241 in China. Chile and Peru together supply 6,335 kt CC of China's consumption, 1.49 times the 4,254 kt CC they ship to China directly as concentrate; the rest arrives as cathode refined elsewhere or as anode through third countries (B3).
