## 1. What the round did

**M.1.** `build_ore_grade.region_bands` applied the ±10 % margin to each region's **30-year series
extremes**, so Chile's ceiling was 1.1 × its *1995* grade — 75 % above its 2019 nominal — and bound
in 26 of 30 years. The band is now year-local, `g(t)·(1 ∓ margin)`; `region_bands` survives as the
σ source only. ADR 0023's wider carried margin (±20 %) is preserved, re-based on the carried year's
own grade. Tailings bands untouched — measured not to bind (Chile 0/30 at either edge, both routes).

**M.2.** The global concentrate band [0.20, 0.35] → **[0.24, 0.33]**, nominal unchanged at 0.275.
Schlesinger's 20–35 % is the range of an *individual* concentrate; every model edge is a region-year
average over all shipments and cannot sit at that envelope's edge.

## 3. And it fails, in the most informative way available

**The pressure moved three times.** Each band is a wall; the infeasibility is a fluid that finds the
next lowest one.

1. *Base*: it escapes through **Chile's ore-grade ceiling** — discard rock, keep copper.
2. *M.1* closes that: it escapes through **Chile's concentrate floor** — legs pinned at 0.20 rise
   from 35 % to 55 % of Chile's export leg-years, and Chile's mine output falls 0.655 → 0.533.
3. *M.2* closes that: the legs press on the **new** floor harder still — **66 %** at 0.24, with
   Chile→Japan at 100 % in every variant — and it escapes through **United States ore**, whose
   retention goes 0.990 → **1.356**, about **+1.0 Gt of fabricated ore** in a region that had been
   matching its feed almost exactly.

## 4. What this establishes

The round was designed to test whether the bands *cause* the deletion. They do not. They **shape**
it. Closing them redistributes the same contradiction and, on the copper side, redistributes it
worse.

That is the direct experimental confirmation of the amplification the Chile diagnosis could only
hypothesise: Chile's concentrate node is over-determined by 3,053 kt CC and the posterior destroys
13,124 — 4.3×. Round M shows where the other 10,000 goes when you take one hiding place away: to
the next one. The real target is the **discharge path** — why a contradiction between two ICSG
figures is paid for by deleting the trade and the ore that connect them, rather than by the
anchors that disagree. Chile's export legs carry cv 0.069 after ADR 0017 damping; its scale-total
anchors carry cv 0.017. That ratio, not any band, decides who pays.

## 5. Decision

Recommended: **revert both, and make the discharge path the next round.**

- The round fails four of its own pre-agreed criteria, two of them physical.
- It converts deletion into fabrication: the US chain was correct (0.990) and is now inventing
  1.0 Gt of ore. A different falsehood of similar size is not progress, and the −12 % total
  deviation is the net of a 1.30 Gt gain at Chile against a 1.00 Gt loss at the US.
- Round L already shipped over a failed exporter stop rule, on the argument that the failure was a
  symptom of the Chile defect. Round M attacked that defect and made the symptom **worse**
  (0.574 → 0.467). Shipping again over the same failed criterion would compound the first call
  rather than vindicate it.

**Kept regardless of the decision**, because none of it depends on the band values:

- the **σ-decoupling mechanism** (`unc_type="sd"` with an explicit `value_sd`) — correct on its own
  terms, inert when the band reverts, and the reason this round is attributable at all;
- the **attribution gate** and `roundM_grade_band_diag.py`;
- the finding, and the year-local band itself as a *known-good option* to revisit once the
  discharge path is fixed — at which point it should be re-measured, not assumed.

**The open question the round raises and does not answer:** whether the failure is "year-local" or
"too tight". Chile's ceiling went from 1.66 % to 1.10 %; a wider year-local margin (±25 %, ceiling
1.25 %) would still forbid the 1.66 % fabrication while leaving more room than ±10 %. That is one
run, and it is the only cheap variant left in this family.

## 6. Decision (2026-09-16): reverted, and the revert verified

The owner took the recommendation. **ADR 0028 ships the σ split and rejects both band changes.**
`ore_grade` **v7** and `conc` **v8** restore the v4/v6 values exactly (nominal, `value_min`,
`value_max` all equal; σ to 1e-16) with only `unc_type = "sd"` differing.

Verification run `opt_20260916_124721_c4d1d944`:

| | sum-row z² | gate vs base |
|---|---|---|
| base `opt_20260915_131115_b8bf05a9` | 1,720,192 | — |
| revert `opt_20260916_124721_c4d1d944` | **1,719,806** | `max|Δf_agg| 0`, `max rel Δσ_agg 7.5e-16`, `Δcoverage 0` |

**A useful by-product: the solver's reproducibility floor.** Those two runs are semantically
identical — Stage 2 is bit-identical and the only input difference is a σ perturbation at 7.5e-16
relative — yet the headline differs by **386, or 0.022 %**. That is Clarabel's interior-point path
responding to a machine-epsilon change, and it calibrates every number in the arc: **a sum-row z²
move below roughly 0.02 % is noise.** Round M's +2.0 % is ninety times that, so the rejection rests
on signal; and by the same token no future round should claim a sub-0.05 % improvement without
saying how it is distinguished from this floor.

Shipping state: `ore_grade` v7, `conc` v8, the σ split, the attribution gate and
`roundM_grade_band_diag.py`. The rejected implementation stays in commit `f6bd5c9`.
