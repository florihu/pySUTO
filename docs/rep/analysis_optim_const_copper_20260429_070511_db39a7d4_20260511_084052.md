## 2 — Does the solution look sensible?

**Yes, mostly — with caveats.**

[sd_t_05_distribution_of_a.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_t_05_distribution_of_a.png) shows the |a*| distribution is bimodal in every year:
- a "physical" lobe centred around 10⁻⁵ … 10⁻⁴ (technical coefficients, small shares),
- a "stock/flow" lobe centred around 10² … 10³ (tonnes),
- and a sharp spike at the p95 (~10²·⁵). The spike doesn't move much across years, which is *good* — it means the bulk of the SUT structure stays stable.

[sd_t_03_solution_vs_prior.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_t_03_solution_vs_prior.png) — `(a* − a₀)/σ_a` p95 sits around **44** in every year and the relative `|a* − a₀|/|a₀|` p95 starts at 24.9 (1995) and drops to **6–7.5** later. **This is concerning**: even with warm-start, ~5 % of cells move by more than 6× their prior magnitude. Combined with the |z| tail on `sum_eq`, this signals the solver is being asked to over-fit a *small* fraction of cells to absorb regional inconsistencies.

[sd_t_06_cstar_vs_c.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_t_06_cstar_vs_c.png) — relative `(c* − c)/(|c|+δ)` has p95 ≤ 8.95e8 with median 12–97. Outliers there reflect rows where `c≈0` (the +δ floor dominates) — interpret with care.

[sd_r_04_hard_active.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_r_04_hard_active.png) — Type-4 ratio-bound activity peaks at ~12 % in 1999 then settles below 2 %. **Strange developments:**
- The objective `f*` is monotonically *increasing* (becoming less negative) over time: −1.18e8 → −9.36e7 ([sd_r_01_solver_health.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_r_01_solver_health.png), top panel). This is consistent with warm-start + slack penalty: as the prior `a₀` gets closer to the data, less slack is needed, so the (negative) entropy-style term costs less in magnitude. Not pathological by itself.
- `res_primal_inf` and `res_dual_inf` (the infeasibility certificates, see [registry.json](../data/proc/opt/registry.json)) drift **upwards** with time (`res_dual_inf` 1.5e-3 in 1995 → 1.06e-2 in 2024). Combined with all 30 timesteps stopping at exactly 200 iterations, this is a clear sign of the solver hitting the iteration cap before reaching `ε_abs/ε_rel`, *not* of any year being more infeasible than another.

## 5 — Why do the `|a*|` and `|c*|` distributions evolve over time?

[sd_r_07_time_hued.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_r_07_time_hued.png) shows the colour-graded histograms. Three things are happening:

1. **Warm-start contraction.** From t=1996 onward, `a₀(t) = a*(t−1)`. Each year inherits the previous solution and only has to absorb the *new* year's `c(t)` change — so successive `a*` lie progressively closer to one another. This is visible as the histogram bars piling up at narrower bins as the hue moves from purple (1995) to yellow (2024).
2. **Slack budget shrinks.** Because `a*(t−1)` already satisfies most of the prior year's `sum_eq`, residual ε(t) → ε(t−1) plus a small ∆-correction. ‖ε‖∞ falls from ~10⁶·⁵ (1995) to ~10⁵·⁴ (2024) ([sd_r_03_soft_slack.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_r_03_soft_slack.png)). The fraction of `sum_eq` rows breaching 3σ falls from 12.3 % → 8.4 %.
3. **Mode separation persists.** The bimodal shape of |a*| (small-ratio lobe + large-flow lobe) does **not** collapse over time — the *structure* of the system stays the same, only the *spread within each mode* tightens.

For `c*` (also bimodal, with a sharp peak near `c*≈0` for the many trivial rows): the tail shrinks year-on-year, again because warm-start carries forward most of the calibration.

## 6 — Why does `‖a* − a₀‖` shrink over the 30 timesteps?

[sd_r_05_prior_posterior.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_r_05_prior_posterior.png) shows the prior-posterior distance falling by ~3 orders of magnitude.

- **1995 is cold-started**: `a₀(1995)` is a flat / uniform prior, so the solver has to move the entire SUT structure from "no information" to "fits the 1995 data" → enormous distance (L₂ ≈ 1.1e7, L∞ ≈ 6.6e5).
- **1996 onward is warm-started**: `a₀(t) = a*(t−1)`. The new year's data only differs from last year's solution by the genuine year-to-year flow changes in the world, plus measurement-noise updates. So `‖a* − a₀‖` collapses by ~10× immediately and keeps tapering as the system reaches a moving-average steady state (relative L₂ stabilises around 0.04 by 2020).
- The remaining slow downward trend after 2000 reflects that *the same set of cells keeps moving each year* — the model is gradually converging on the cells that the data continuously contradicts (the same CSXEW/ROW rows from §1).

The warm-start delta ([sd_r_06_warmstart_delta.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260429_070511_db39a7d4_20260511_084052/diag/sd_r_06_warmstart_delta.png)) tells the same story from a different angle: rel-L₂(t → t+1) drops from 0.4 (1996) to ~0.04 (2024).

## Recommendations

### A — Things to troubleshoot

1. **Investigate the CSXEW `sum_eq` rows.**
   - Audit `c` and `σ_c` for the 11 region rows in §1a (`CSXEW | ROW, Mongolia, China, Iran, Australia, Peru, Mexico, Zambia, US, Chile, Russia`).
   - The 25–60 σ violations probably reflect either (i) an over-tight `σ_c` (under-stated uncertainty) on a thin-data sector, or (ii) a `c` that is structurally incompatible with the supply side — e.g. SX/EW capacity in the data feed exceeds the matte/concentrate route the model leaves available.
   - Spot-check: load the contributing sub-entity rows for one region (e.g. Mongolia, where China is a near neighbour) and verify the upstream flows (mining → concentrate → SXEW) match the published SX/EW production.

2. **Audit the "ROW" aggregation.**
   - Every sector hits its worst z-score on the ROW bucket. Either split ROW into 2–3 sub-regions (Africa-ex-DRC, Asia-ex-named, Other-Americas) or **inflate `σ_c`** on every ROW row to reflect the heterogeneity that's been hidden by aggregation.

3. **Check the Mining/Ore coupling.**
   - The two sectors have *identical* mean|z| in §1a — they share underlying flows, so any tightening of one feeds the other. Confirm the row-level coupling is intended and that we're not double-counting a constraint.

4. **Investigate the Cathode | US and India outliers.**
   - The largest absolute |ε| in the `sum_eq` heatmap is on `Cathode|India` and `CP|India`; the largest z-score on Cathode is the **United States** (~68σ max). These two rows probably encode the largest secondary/recycling contributions in the feed and may be carrying mis-attributed scrap flows.

5. **Decide whether `AlmostSolved` is acceptable.**
   - All 30 timesteps stop at the 200-iter cap with `gap_rel ≤ 4e-6` and machine-precision `res_primal`. The model is **effectively converged**; the residual `res_*_inf` is just the certificate not vanishing. Either accept it explicitly (relax solver tolerances) or raise the iteration cap to confirm Clarabel can ever stamp `Solved`.

### B — Changes to solver initialisation

1. **Raise `max_iter` to 400** for a confirmation run on 1995, 2010, 2024. If status flips to `Solved`, we know the cap is the only obstacle. If it stays `AlmostSolved` with step-length still shrinking, the limit is data conflict, not iteration budget.
2. **Loosen `eps_abs` / `eps_rel`** to e.g. 1e-7 (default is typically 1e-8). Given `gap_rel ≈ 1e-6`, this would let the solver declare success cleanly.
3. **Improve the cold-start (1995) prior.** Instead of a uniform `a₀(1995)`, use the global average of `a*` over 1996–2010 (or a simple regression-imputed point) so that 1995 doesn't carry the huge L∞-distance penalty. This cuts the worst iter-count year.
4. **Per-row σ_c scaling for ROW.** Multiply `σ_c` on every constraint whose region is `ROW` by 3–5 to reflect the aggregation uncertainty. This should pull the worst-z tail in §1 inside `±3σ` without touching the constraint structure.
5. **Equality-quadratic regularisation.** The growing `res_dual_inf` (1.5e-3 → 1e-2) suggests the dual is slightly unbounded along a direction — a small quadratic regulariser on the slack vector (e.g. ε^T D ε with D = 1e-6·I) typically tightens this without biasing the primal.
6. **Iterative-refinement of the linear solver.** `qdldl` is the QDLDL Cholesky path; consider switching to MKL Pardiso or HSL MA57 if available — the nnz(L)/nnz(A) ratio (~0.93) suggests minimal fill-in, so the bottleneck is iteration count, not factorisation, which means a more accurate linear solve per iteration could shave 10–30 iterations.
