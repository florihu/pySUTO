For these `a_0 = 0` edges, σ_a still gets a meaningful value from the FlatPrior 3-level scale cascade in [`flat_prior.py:283-316`](../../src/pysuto/impute/flat_prior.py#L283-L316):

| valuation | n (a_0 = 0) | σ_a min | σ_a median | σ_a p95 | σ_a max |

## 3 — Upstream investigation B: per-row unit audit of `G`

The audit walks every row of `G` and lists the valuation buckets touched by its non-zero columns (using `acc.edge_index["valuation"]` for column → valuation lookup). Results:

| row family | rows | mixed Mass+CC | pct |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **sum_eq** (soft, free) | 19,740 | **19,740** | **100.0%** |
| **mass_balance** (hard, zero) | 19,740 | **19,740** | **100.0%** |
| ratio_eq (soft, free) | 435,690 | 50,000+ in 50k sample (likely ~100%) | ≈100% |

**Every soft sum_eq row mixes Mass and CC edges. Every hard mass_balance row mixes Mass and CC edges.** This is the structural defect: when a row sums edges of both valuations with bare ±1 coefficients, it is implicitly asserting that one tonne of gross ore weight is *interchangeable* with one tonne of Cu content. The ~1000× scale gap makes the Mass edges dominate the LHS arithmetically, even though the RHS target `c` is in a single valuation unit.

### 3a — Forensic: Mining\|ROW sum_eq at t=1995 (row 19786, constraint_id `sub_entity_node93_t1995`)

`c = +38,599.9 t (Cu content)`, `σ_c = 3,282.0` (CV = 0.085).

The G row's non-zero columns at t=1995:

| edge_id | coef | from\|region | to | val | a_0 (t) | coef·a_0 |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| 2760 | +1.0 | CO\|ROW | Mining | **Mass** | 24,009.4 | +24,009.4 |
| 2761 | +1.0 | CO\|ROW | Mining | **CC** | 481.2 | +481.2 |
| 8340 | -1.0 | Mining\|ROW | WR | **Mass** | **1,377,200** | **-1,377,200** |
| 8341 | -1.0 | Mining\|ROW | WR | **CC** | 344.3 | -344.3 |

**`Σ coef·a_0 = -1,353,053` vs `c = +38,600`.** The Mass component alone contributes **1,401,209** to `|LHS|` while the CC component contributes only **825.5** — a ratio of **1,700×**.

The row, as written in `G`, encodes: *"(CO→Mining Mass) + (CO→Mining CC) − (Mining→WR Mass) − (Mining→WR CC) = 38,600 (Cu content)."* This is unit-incoherent: subtracting 1.4 M t of gross-ore-weight tailings from 24 kt of incoming concentrated ore *should not equal* 38.6 kt of Cu — it makes no physical sense and no choice of `F*` can make it true unless the Mass edges shrink to zero.

The solver finds the cheapest way out: drive both Mass edges to 0, leaving `LHS = 481.2 − 344.3 = 136.9` — well within `±3·σ_c = ±9,846` of the target. The Mass-edge regulariser cost (5% CV anchor at a_0 = 1.377 M t) is `≈ 400` per collapsed edge — vastly cheaper than the `(1,400,000/3,282)² ≈ 180,000` sum_eq cost of keeping `F ≈ a_0`.

### 3b — Forensic: Ore\|ROW sum_eq at t=1995 (row 29656, constraint_id `sub_entity_node187_t1995`)

`c = +38,599.9 t (Cu)`, `σ_c = 3,282`.

| edge_id | coef | from\|region | to | val | a_0 (t) |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| 13980 | +1.0 | Ore\|ROW | MF | Mass | **0** (FlatPrior) |
| 13981 | +1.0 | Ore\|ROW | MF | CC | **0** (FlatPrior) |
| 14040 | +1.0 | Ore\|ROW | SXEW | Mass | 5,118.8 |
| 14041 | +1.0 | Ore\|ROW | SXEW | CC | 46.1 |

`Σ coef·a_0 = 5,165`, target c = 38,600. The two FlatPrior'd Mass edges cannot anchor anything, and the lone substantial Mass edge (5,119 t) is overshadowed by being summed alongside CC. This row *can never* satisfy `c = 38,600` because the available a_0 is only 5,165 even at its mass scale — and that's before the same coefficient-1 mixing as row 19786 forces the Mass edges down. This row is the prior REP's `z = -11.76` wall on Ore|ROW.

### 3c — Sign of the structural bug

The bug appears in *all 19,740 sum_eq and mass_balance rows*. It is not specific to ROW or to any year — it is how the constraint builder writes `G`. Two equally valid root causes are possible:

(a) **The `c` target is wrong.** The data ingestion may be putting Cu-content totals into a constraint that physically should be summing gross weights; or
(b) **The `G` coefficients are wrong.** Mass-valuation columns should be multiplied by the local Cu grade (~0.005–0.02) when entering a Cu-content row.

Inspection of `init_copper_acc.py` / the SumConstraintBuilder.from_acc_struct entry path is the next step. Either way, the *symptom* is identical: every row asks Mass-edges to play a Cu-content role with no unit conversion. See §7 for the remediation options.

## 5 — Scaling discussion: why Clarabel-side rescaling cannot fix this

The user's question was *"what can I do so that Clarabel accepts such scale differences via prior scaling?"* The honest answer: **no scaling intervention can repair a unit-incoherent constraint.** Three candidate interventions and the verdict on each:

### 5a — Internal equilibration (already on)

[`solver.py:170-178`](../../src/pysuto/solver.py#L170-L178) leaves Clarabel's default static + dynamic regularisation enabled, which includes Ruiz equilibration of `P`, `A`, `b`. This is doing its job — the May-21 run achieves `res_dual ≈ 2.5e-11` (excellent KKT conditioning). The 1000× spread in column magnitudes does not break the linear algebra. **Equilibration helps numerics, not modelling.**

### 5b — Variable rescaling `a_j' = a_j / s_j`

A natural substitute is `a_j' = a_j / s_j` with `s_j = max(|a_0(t=0,j)|, σ_a_floor)`, so the QP sees O(1) decision variables and the prior penalty becomes `((a_j'-1)/CV_j)²`. This makes the *prior* CV-uniform — useful — but the constraint becomes `Σ_j G_{kj}·s_j·a_j' = c_k`, with `s_j` differing by 1000× between Mass and CC columns. The Mass columns now multiply `a_j'` by `s_j ≈ 10⁶` and CC by `s_j ≈ 10³` — the *constraint coefficients* now span the same 1000× range. The solver still has to make Mass terms vanish to satisfy the row. **Variable rescaling is a numerical pre-conditioning, not a fix for the row's physical incoherence.**

### 5c — Row rescaling `G_{k,:} /= |c_k|`

Dividing the row by `|c_k|` turns σ_c / |c| into the natural quantity (already ~0.117). It does not change the *direction* of the constraint or the relative weights of Mass vs CC. **Same verdict — cosmetic; doesn't fix mixing.**

### 5d — Tighter σ_a (CV uniform, e.g. cv_min 0.05 → 0.20)

This would pull F harder back to a_0 — but the §3a forensic shows that *even* at 5% CV (the tightest realistic anchor), the regulariser cost of collapse (~400) is dwarfed by the sum_eq cost of staying (~180,000). Raising cv_min to 0.20 would reduce the collapse cost to `(1/0.20)² = 25` per edge — yet the sum_eq cost only drops linearly via better fit, not enough to flip the calculus. **Marginal effect; would over-anchor edges where data legitimately calls for movement.**

### 5e — The real diagnosis

The problem is *not* a scale mismatch Clarabel cannot digest. The problem is **a constraint that asks 1 t of gross ore weight to equal 1 t of Cu content**. No amount of preconditioning, equilibration, or σ-tuning can make that constraint consistent. The fix has to land in `G`.

## 7 — Improvement recommendations (priority-ordered)

### (I) Repair unit-incoherent `G` rows — the structural fix

Every sum_eq and mass_balance row currently sums Mass + CC edges with bare ±1 coefficients (§3). **Two equally defensible reformulations**, pick one based on what `c` is intended to represent:

**Option I-A: Separate Mass and CC sum_eq rows entirely.** Build two parallel constraint families — `sum_eq_mass` rows whose RHS is the gross-weight total, `sum_eq_cc` rows whose RHS is the Cu-content total. Each row references only its own valuation. Clean, easy to audit, but doubles the soft-block row count (38 k → 77 k rows). Mass-balance follows the same split.

**Option I-B: Multiply Mass-valuation coefficients in G by local Cu grade.** Keep one sum_eq row per node, but the row reads `Σ(grade_e · F_e^{Mass}) + Σ(F_e^{CC}) = c (Cu content)` — i.e. Mass contributions are converted to Cu-equivalent at row-build time. Requires a per-edge grade lookup, but preserves the dual-accounting structure the rest of the pipeline already assumes.

Implementation site: `SumConstraintBuilder.from_acc_struct` and `SumConstraintBuilder.from_csv` in [`src/pysuto/constraints/sum.py`](../../src/pysuto/constraints/sum.py); `MassBalanceBuilder.build_static` in [`src/pysuto/constraints/mass_balance.py`](../../src/pysuto/constraints/mass_balance.py).

Either fix collapses the §3a/§3b walls and **restores F\*(t) to roughly track a_0(t)** at the Mass scale. This is the single intervention with leverage on the user's symptom #1, #2, and #3 simultaneously.

### (II) Tighten σ_a or remove `a_0 = 0` Mass edges from the soft block

53.6% of edges have `a_0 = 0` from FlatPrior fallback (§2). On these edges, σ_a ≈ 10–300 with no anchor — the solver freely sets F to noise. After (I), these edges still won't be *forced* to a sensible value because the prior is silent. Two options:

- **(II-a)** When FlatPrior fires (`a_0 = 0` and σ_a from the CV·scale cascade), tighten σ_a to `min(σ_a, 0.10·scale(j))` instead of `0.30·scale(j)` — relies more on the data and less on the imputer.
- **(II-b)** Exclude `a_0 = 0` edges from soft-block rows entirely (they carry no information), keeping them only in hard mass_balance and bound rows. This shrinks the free cone but doesn't change the structural picture.

Recommended: **start with (II-a)**, smaller risk.

### (III) Build-time assertion: warn on mixed-valuation rows

Two-line guard in `SumConstraintBuilder`, `RatioConstraintBuilder`, `MassBalanceBuilder`: at row creation, check the valuations of the included edges. If more than one valuation appears, *and* coefficients are bare ±1 (no grade multiplier), log a warning. Prevents re-introduction of the same class of bug after fix (I).

### (IV) Do not implement variable rescaling now — defer

`a_j' = a_j / s_j` would be a useful numerical guardrail once (I) lands, but applying it *before* the model fix would obscure the diagnostic signal (F* in original units immediately reveals collapses). Defer until (I) and (II) ship.

### Priority order

1. **(I-A or I-B)** repair `G` rows — the only fix that closes the user's three symptoms simultaneously.
2. **(III)** add the build-time assertion so the bug cannot silently return.
3. **(II-a)** tighten FlatPrior σ_a — secondary, takes effect once (I) lets the network breathe.
4. **(IV)** defer numerical rescaling.

Recommendation **(A1)** from the May-21 REP (inflate σ_c on ROW sum_eq) should be **dropped** — §4 shows σ_c is well-sized and the wall persists regardless.
