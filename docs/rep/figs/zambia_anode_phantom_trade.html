Removing the three hub destinations returns Zambia's export-to-output ratio to 0.25–0.74 in every year from 2016 on — physically sane throughout.

1 · Recorded exports break through the physical ceiling in 2016

Zambia's smelters set a hard ceiling on how much anode it can ship. Until 2015 exports track well under it. In 2016 a new destination class appears and the stack crosses the line.

2 · The copper goes in and never comes out

If these were genuine transhipment points, what arrives would leave again. It does not. Across 2016–2024 the three jurisdictions absorb 4,541 kt Cu and re-export 39 kt — and ICSG reports no refined production for any of them, so it cannot have been consumed there either.

3 · China's imports from Zambia were never diverted

This is where the hypothesis needs amending. If the hub legs were the China cargo re-labelled, China's recorded imports from Zambia would fall as the hub legs rise. They do the opposite — 206 → 381 kt Cu — and China books almost nothing from the hubs (under 1 kt/yr throughout).

What this rules out, and what it leaves

Congolese copper is here — as ore, and it is already inside the ceiling. Zambia is the export corridor for Congo D.R., and Congolese concentrate genuinely feeds Zambian smelters: 190 kt Cu in 2013, 196 kt Cu in 2017, 73 kt Cu in 2022. That is exactly why Zambia's smelter output (788 kt Cu in 2017) exceeds its own mine output (628 kt Cu). But the smelting happens in Zambia, so ICSG already counts it as Zambian smelter production — it raises the ceiling rather than creating room above it.

Finished metal does not transit. Recorded Congo→Zambia anode and cathode are 0.0 kt in every year of the series. Congo ships its own refined copper out directly — 2,041 kt Cu in 2024, of which 1,447 kt straight to China. And Zambia imports essentially no anode itself (0.00–0.16 kt/yr), so there is no re-export route into the anode line either.

Not a re-routing of the China flow. China's imports from Zambia rose from 206 to 381 kt Cu straight through the break.

A second anomaly, older and unexplained. Counted in contained copper, Zambia's total recorded exports exceeded everything its plants turned out well before 2016 — 1.59× in 2013, on the cathode line, with no hub tonnage in play. The anode story here does not explain that one, and Congolese cathode transit cannot either (it is zero). It deserves its own look.

What remains. The hub legs have no physical backing at Zambia's end — the smelters never made that copper, Congolese feed included. The legitimate destinations are still recorded separately and at plausible volumes, so this reads as the same cargo booked twice: once to the buyer, once to the trader's jurisdiction of invoice. The data cannot say which of the two records is the duplicate, and it cannot trace where each shipment physically landed. For the reconciliation the distinction does not matter: roughly 450–960 kt/yr of anode is being asserted that Zambia did not produce.
