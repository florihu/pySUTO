0 · The 2024 system, before and after reconciliation

Contained copper, world total. The reconciliation keeps the scrap, keeps the cathode, and takes the difference out of the mine: 22.8 → 18.4 Mt at milling.

1 · Secondary copper: 17 %, flat — and double-counted in the model

ICSG's own series put secondary refined production at 4.7 Mt in 2024, 17.2 % of refined output including SX-EW, and that share has been 0.170 → 0.172 since 2010 while output grew 44 %. Recycling has kept pace with growth, not gained on it — consistent with ICSG's ~16 % recycling input rate and with end-of-life studies that cap EoL supply below half of demand even in 2050.

The model's posterior shows 30 %. The reason is in the parse: ICSG's secondary-smelter series and its secondary-refined series are both converted to inputs, but secondary refined production already includes the copper that came through secondary smelters as blister and anode. Austria's Brixlegg — a 100 % secondary plant — is the proof: 60 kt secondary smelter production inside 123 kt secondary refined, booked by the feed as 187 kt of scrap against a 123 kt refined anchor, a 148 % secondary share. The solver deletes Austria's smelter scrap to 0.1 kt.

Why this is the mine-chain deletion. With both series booked, the anode anchor can absorb 14,758 kt of concentrate in 2024 against 18,207 kt mined — 3,450 kt has nowhere to go. Nested, the gap is −652 kt. Over 30 years the double count is 74.2 Mt; the model deletes 52.9 Mt of mine output (ratio 0.71, r = 0.64).

Who pays, 2020–24

The surplus is not paid where it arises. China's smelters lose 14.9 Mt of imported concentrate and take on 7.0 Mt of scrap — their posterior scrap share sits on the ceiling of their hard band — and the exporters that fed those imports lose the mine output.

Concerns the numbers carry

ADR 0021's "not a double count" holds for secondary refined against the total anchor; it was never checked for the smelter series. Direct-melt scrap used by fabricators is outside the model boundary, so "secondary share" here is refinery-side only. Scrap is untraded in the model (HS 7404 is not in the BACI map), so regional secondary input is where ICSG says scrap was used, not where it arose. The posterior's rising secondary share since 2020 is the reconciliation filling China's smelters up to the band, not a trend.

2 · The hinge moved to China, and the books do not close across it

China's share of world smelter concentrate intake went from 7 % in 1995 to 42 % in 2024 (49 % in the prior), and 81 % of that intake is imported. Chile and Peru still mine ~30 % of the world's copper and smelt 9 % of it. The IEA's 2026 Outlook gives the industrial side: China holds 50 % of smelting capacity, benchmark treatment charges settled at USD 0 in 2026, and smelters outside China run below 70 % utilisation.

This link is exactly where the reconciliation discharges: import-dependent smelters lose 20–35 % of their intake, self-sufficient ones hold. Half of the world's mine-chain deletion is arithmetically forced before the solver starts, by holes in the BACI concentrate matrix: Chile's recorded exports halve and double year on year while its mine output is flat, so in 19 of 30 years its copper cannot leave at any grade in the physical band.

3 · The stock moved east

China's share of refined copper put into final use rose from 11 % (1995) to 58 % (2024); the EU-14, the US and Japan/Korea/Taiwan together fell from 63 % to 22 %. Cumulatively 221 of 559 Mt — 40 % — went to China, 104 Mt in the 2010s alone. The end-of-life scrap of the 2030s will arise where the copper was put into use; China's 2019 import restrictions already re-routed the world's scrap trade through South-East Asia. A model in which scrap is untraded cannot see any of that.

A concern, not a finding. The stock-change nodes are used as buffers: cumulative exchange-stock additions are 15.8 Mt in the posterior against 5.8 in the prior, sitting in the LME warehouse countries (UK 2.6, Singapore 2.1, Netherlands 2.0 Mt) where no exchange ever accumulated that much.

What this sends next

Round O — the secondary double count. A parse-level fix so that scrap charged to refineries carries only the direct route, Scrap→CFR = (S_ref − S_sm·eff_CFR·eff_ER)/(eff_CFR·eff_ER), with the smelter route unchanged. Acceptance before any solver run: Austria's share → 1.0 and the world concentrate gap → 0; on the chain: the mine-chain deletion far below today's 12.8 %, the posterior secondary share ≈ 0.17, China's smelters off their band ceiling, Chile's mines recovering with no change to the grade cones. The export-mass gap is re-measured after that; it is the regional routing of this surplus.
