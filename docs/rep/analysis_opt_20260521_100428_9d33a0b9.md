### 2c — Worst row → edges: why is `c* ≈ 0` when 104 kt is observed?

**Row 19,457 — Ore | ROW | 2024:**

`c = 104,062 t (Cu content)`, `σ_c = 8,847`, `c* = 2.6e-4 ≈ 0`. The 4 outflow edges of `Ore|ROW`:

| edge | from → to | val | a₀ | σ_a | l_b | F* |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 14038 | Ore\|ROW → MF\|ROW | Mass | 0.0 | 10,658.07 | 0 | **1.3e-4** |
| 14039 | Ore\|ROW → MF\|ROW | CC | 0.0 | 95.92 | 0 | **1.3e-4** |
| 14098 | Ore\|ROW → SXEW\|ROW | Mass | 3,350.0 | 167.7 | 0 | **6.7e-10** |
| 14099 | Ore\|ROW → SXEW\|ROW | CC | 30.15 | 2.24 | 0 | **1.0e-9** |

All four edges are at their **flow lower bound (`l_b = 0`)**. The σ_a is now sensible (10658 / 95 / 168 / 2.2), not the 1e-6 of the prior run.

**This is no longer a σ_a-pin wall** — it is a **rational tradeoff** by the solver:

- Cost of picking `F = 0` on all 4 outflows:
  - Regulariser on the SXEW Mass edge: `(3350/167.7)² ≈ 400`
  - Regulariser on the SXEW CC edge: `(30.15/2.24)² ≈ 181`
  - Soft sum_eq penalty: `z² ≈ 138`
  - **Total ≈ 719**
- Cost of picking `F ≈ a₀` (which fits the regulariser but not the sum_eq):
  - The four outflows together absorb at most `(3350 + 0 + 0 + 0) ≈ 3,350 t (Cu)` against the observed `104,062 t` — leaves a residual of ~100 kt
  - Soft sum_eq penalty: `(100,712 / 8847)² ≈ 130`
  - Plus the regulariser would need a₀ to shift by 100kt — a₀ is 0 / 3350 / 30 / 0 — so to *actually* close c, F would need to be `>> a₀` and the regulariser cost grows quadratically.

The deeper issue is **a units / scale mismatch between the sum_eq RHS and the a₀ scale on Mass edges**:

- `c` on the Ore|ROW sum_eq is **Cu content in tonnes (104 kt)**.
- `a₀` on the `Ore|ROW → MF|ROW (Mass)` edge is **0** (no observation, flat prior), and on `Ore|ROW → SXEW|ROW (Mass)` is **3,350 t** — orders of magnitude smaller than the Cu-content total in the sum_eq.
- Looking at the same forensic for **Mining|ROW 1995**:

| edge | from → to | val | a₀ | F* |
|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 8340 | Mining\|ROW → WR\|ROW | Mass | 1,377,200 | 2.7e-11 (at l_b) |
| 8341 | Mining\|ROW → WR\|ROW | CC | 344.3 | 1.6e-10 (at l_b) |
| 8400 | Mining\|ROW → Ore\|ROW | Mass | 38,255.6 | 6.4e-3 (at l_b) |
| 8401 | Mining\|ROW → Ore\|ROW | CC | 344.3 | 6.4e-3 (at l_b) |

The Mining|ROW → WR|ROW Mass edge has `a₀ = 1.377 M t` (a₀ in **gross ore weight**) versus the soft sum_eq target on Mining|ROW of `c = 38.6 kt` (in **Cu content**). The solver cannot satisfy both `c` (Cu) and `a₀` (Mass) simultaneously because they speak different units. Pinning the Mass edge at `l_b = 0` is the lower-cost choice.

**Net diagnosis.** The Ore-ROW and Mining-ROW walls are now a **valuation-mismatch artifact** between Mass (gross-weight) edges and Cu-content soft constraints, not a σ_a pin and not a missing-edge structural problem. The 4 outflows exist; the network is connected; the regulariser just prefers to sit at the flow lower bound rather than absorb a mass-scaled regulariser cost to satisfy a content-scaled soft constraint.

**Reading.**

- **Concentration-ratio families (conc_*) are dominated by upper-bound binding.** `conc_co_supply` and `conc_ore_use` upper bind 99.7–100% while lower bind <1%. The data wants the *ceiling* of the allowed concentration envelope — the solver is choosing the most concentrated route through each step.
- **Symmetric families exist** (`conc_concentrate_supply`, `conc_matte_supply`, `conc_anode_supply`, `conc_cathode_supply` all 96–100% on both sides). For these the band is *narrow* — upper and lower nearly coincide and both bind simultaneously. This is structurally how `ratio_bound` represents a near-point estimate when `R_min ≈ R_max`.
- **Efficiency-ratio families (eff_*) look very different.** `eff_smelting`, `eff_cfr` upper bound effectively inactive; `eff_er` is roughly 50/50. These ratios live well inside the allowed envelope — the bounds aren't binding because the soft constraints don't drive them to the boundary.
- **Stock bounds (net_stock_bound_*)** are asymmetric, with `usc` (used-stock) and `mesc` (manufacturing-end-stock) regularly at their upper limits. This means cumulative stock is being driven to its physical maximum more years than not.

**Is 90%+ binding a bug or a feature?** Mostly a *feature with one concerning facet*:

- The quadratic objective is symmetric in F-space — it doesn't bias toward upper bounds geometrically. The asymmetry comes entirely from *where the regulariser target a₀ and the soft-fit target sit relative to the bounds*.
- For families with `R_min ≈ R_max` (tight bands), both sides being active is structurally inevitable: that is just how the constraint reads "R must equal X" via a pair of inequalities.
- For families with a real band where only the upper binds (the `conc_co_supply` / `conc_ore_use` pattern), the upper bound is doing useful work: it is the only thing stopping the solver from concentrating all metal through the most efficient single path. Removing it would over-concentrate.
- The one mildly concerning facet: `net_stock_bound_usc_<region> upper` binding 90–100% suggests these caps may be set tighter than what the data actually wants. Either the stock caps are conservative (true upper isn't 100% utilised in reality) or the imputation a₀ is unrealistically high. Worth verifying against the underlying USGS/national-statistics data.

## 7 — Improvement recommendations (upstream)

### A — Ore/Mining ROW wall is now a Mass-vs-CC valuation mismatch

The `z = −11.76` wall on Ore|ROW (2024) and Mining|ROW (1995) is no longer a σ_a pin. The forensic (§2c) shows the solver picks `F = 0` on the 4 Ore|ROW outflows because:

- the soft sum_eq is in **Cu-content tonnes** (`c = 104 kt`),
- the Mass-valuation edge regularisers have `a₀` in **gross ore weight** (`Mining|ROW→WR Mass a₀ = 1.377 M t`),
- the two scales aren't comparable, so closing the sum_eq via the Mass edges would cost more regulariser than just paying the soft penalty.

**Fix #1 — Inflate `σ_c` on ROW sum_eq rows specifically.** ROW is an aggregate of dozens of heterogeneous small producers; the BACI/USGS total for "rest of world" carries genuinely higher uncertainty than a country-level total. Current `σ_c` for `Ore|ROW 2024` is 8,847 (CV ≈ 8.5% on `c = 104 kt`). Bumping the CV to **30–50% for ROW-aggregated sum_eq rows** would shrink the z from 11.76 to ~2–3 — defensible because the underlying observation IS less precise. This is the **recommended action**.

Implementation: a single conditional in `SumConstraintBuilder.build_dynamic` of the form

```python
is_row = (node_region == "ROW")
sigma_c[is_row] = np.maximum(sigma_c[is_row], 0.35 * np.abs(c[is_row]))
```

**Fix #2 (deeper) — Audit Mass-vs-CC unit consistency.** The fact that a Mass edge a₀ for Mining|ROW→WR is 1.377M while the Mining sum_eq has c = 38.6 k strongly suggests the two are not in the same units. Check whether `Mass` valuation should be holding gross-ore weight (which is irrelevant to a Cu mass-balance) or Cu-equivalent mass. If gross weight, those edges should not enter the Cu-content sum_eq at all (a coefficient of `Cu_grade × Mass` not `1 × Mass`). This is a deeper investigation in `init_copper_acc.py` / constraint builder.

### B — 90%+ binding ratio bounds: don't substitute with constants, widen asymmetrically

The user's question (a): "should I replace upper/lower ratio bounds with a single constant value?"

**No** — the band is doing useful work even when one side binds 99% of the time. Pinning to a point estimate removes degrees of freedom that the soft `ratio_eq` and `sum_eq` need to balance. Recommended actions:

1. **Loosen the upper-bound side for families with high asymmetry** (`conc_co_supply`, `conc_ore_use`, `conc_anode_use`, `conc_matte_use` all 100% upper-binding / <1% lower-binding). The upper bound is acting as a hard ceiling that the data clearly wants to push through. If the bound came from a physical efficiency limit (e.g. recovery ≤ 99%), verify the limit and bump it up where appropriate.
2. **For `net_stock_bound_*` families** that bind 90%+ on the upper side, investigate whether the stock caps are realistic. Either the data has cumulative stocks that genuinely saturate the caps every year (then the caps are too tight), or the imputation a₀ is over-counting stocks.
3. **Where `R_min ≈ R_max`** (e.g. `conc_concentrate_supply` with 97.99%/99.90%), the pair is operating as a tight point estimate. Leaving it as a band is fine — replacing with a hard equality at the midpoint would save ~440 k constraint rows but would push the burden onto soft constraints elsewhere. Defer until the wall in §A is closed.

### C — Sum_eq vs mass_balance: keep both (Q(b))

The user's question (b): "do the sum_eq constraints make sense given mass_balance already enforces flow conservation by accounting structure?"

**Yes — they are orthogonal.**

- `mass_balance` (hard) enforces `ΣF_in = ΣF_out` at every node — this is *internal physics*, says nothing about the magnitude of flows.
- `sum_eq` (soft) enforces *agreement with external observation* — e.g. BACI says "the total Ore supply for Chile in 2024 was 480 kt" and the constraint reads `Σ(supply edges of Chile|Ore) = 480 kt`.

A solution with all flows = 0 satisfies mass_balance perfectly but ignores every observation. Removing `sum_eq` would let the solver fit any flow pattern that conserves mass — which is exactly the degenerate state we saw with the Ore|ROW row already (F* ≈ 0, mass-balance fine, sum_eq violated). The wall is **caused by** the soft sum_eq being out-leveraged, not by it being redundant.

A possible refinement: at nodes where the per-edge feed data already pins the edge-level flows (`coverage[edges] = 1.0` for every incident edge), the sum_eq is informationally redundant with the edge-level prior. There it could be dropped to reduce the soft-block size. The savings are modest (most nodes have <100% edge coverage), so this is a tier-2 improvement.

### D — ROW inflation (Q(c))

Already covered in §A above as Fix #1. The most cost-effective parametrization change is inflating `σ_c` on ROW sum_eq rows (CV → 30–50%) and **not** the corresponding edge a₀ values. Two-line patch.

### E — Investigate the Mass-valuation gross-weight question

Independent of (A1), the unit mismatch in §2c is a flag worth investigating regardless. If `Mass` edges out of Mining/Ore carry gross-ore tonnage (millions of tonnes), but the Cu-content sum_eq has tens of thousands of tonnes, then either:
- the coefficient in `G` is multiplying the Mass edge by Cu-grade (and Cu-grade is set wrong for ROW — defaulting to 0?), or
- the Mass edge has no place in the Cu-content sum_eq and should be excluded from those rows entirely.

A direct way to test: compute `(G @ F*)` for a Mining|ROW sum_eq row and confirm whether the Mass-edge contribution is multiplied by a grade coefficient (~0.005 for ore) or by 1.0. If by 1.0, the constraint is computing the wrong quantity.

2. **(E) Audit Mass-valuation unit consistency** for Mining and Ore outflows. The forensic in §2c strongly hints at a Cu-grade-vs-gross-weight confusion.
3. **(B1) Loosen upper-bound side for the 4 always-binding ratio families** (`conc_co_supply`, `conc_ore_use`, `conc_anode_use`, `conc_matte_use`).
4. **(B2) Investigate stock-cap calibration** for `net_stock_bound_usc_*` and `net_stock_bound_mesc_*`.
