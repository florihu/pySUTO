## 3 — Where the σ_c numbers come from (constraint pipeline)

Walking the σ_c assembly from [scripts/init_constraints.py](../scripts/init_constraints.py) and [src/pysuto/constraints.py](../src/pysuto/constraints.py):

1. `SumConstraintBuilder.from_acc_struct_subentity` builds one `sub_entity_node{N}_t{Y}` row per (node, year). LHS = +1 on incident edges (positive direction), −1 on Nature/SEM outflows. RHS observation `c` = sum of "supply" edges into the node (or out, for Flow nodes).
2. `sigma_mode = "gaussian"` (default) ⇒ at every timestep, σ_c is recomputed in `build_dynamic`:
   ```python
   c_out[i]   = np.nansum(f_agg[rhs])
   sigma_c[i] = np.sqrt(np.nansum(sigma_agg[rhs] ** 2))
   ```
3. `f_agg` and `sigma_agg` come from the imputation run; for the `_rhs_ids` of `Ore | ROW`, sigma_agg of every contributing edge comes from the same imputer step — and the **independent-noise** sqrt-of-sum-squares assumption collapses σ_c by `√N`.

This is the assembly path. It is correct in code but rests on the assumption that supply-edge errors are **independent**, which is the part that breaks downstream.

| step | imputer | edges filled | cumulative coverage | notes |

| 1 | ParametricImputer (conc_params) | 162,426 | 44.5 % | bulk concentration filler |

| 4 | TimeImputer (linear) | 10,563 | 46.1 % | gap fill |
| 5 | **FlatPriorImputer** | **460,423** | **100 %** | **fills the remaining 54 %** |

Per-feed CVs (`σ / |F|` over observed entries):

| feed | n_obs | CV (p50) |
|---|---|---|
| baci | 41,081 | 3.92 % |
| icsg | 175,279 | 2.26 % |
| crude_ore | 865 | 23.0 % |

Per-edge CV after aggregation (`sigma_agg / |f_agg|` for the 393,929 non-zero entries): median **2.26 %**, p90 3.9 %, p99 23 %.

So far so good. **The break point is Step 5**: `data/input/params/flat_prior_cv.csv`:

```
mask_name, cv,    description
supply,    0.001, "Supply edges are well covered by data feeds; tight prior"
use,       0.3,   "Use edges are sparsely covered; loose prior"
```

**This is the root cause.** A flat CV of **0.001 (0.1 %)** is applied to *every* supply edge that no feed ever touched (54 % of all edges, including the entire `Ore | ROW` outgoing structure and most of the rare-region Ore/Mining outflows). That CV is **~25× tighter than the real feed CVs** (BACI 4 %, ICSG 2.3 %). The imputed σ that ends up in `sigma_agg` for those edges is microscopic; the Gaussian propagation in §3 then collapses it further by √N when many such tight-σ edges enter a single `sum_eq` row.

The bottom panel of [sd_t_03_solution_vs_prior.png](../data/proc/opt/optim_const_copper_20260512_083803_30c16790_20260512_093956/diag/sd_t_03_solution_vs_prior.png) confirms this from the other side: `(a* − a₀) / σ_a` p95 ≈ 84, max **3.0e5**. The solver *wants* to move some `a` cells by 100,000σ but the prior penalty + the tight σ_c keep it pinned.

The description "Supply edges are well covered by data feeds; tight prior" is true *for the supply edges that the feeds touch*, but Step 5 by construction applies it to the supply edges they **don't** touch — precisely the cells where high uncertainty is most plausible.

## 6 — Recommendations for upstream analysis

### A — Imputation: flat-prior CV is the headline issue

1. **Loosen `flat_prior_cv.csv` `supply` CV from 0.001 to a realistic value.** The actual feed CVs (BACI 4 %, ICSG 2.3 %, crude_ore 23 %) say 0.05 (5 %) is the floor; for unobserved supply edges in thin-data regions (`ROW`, Mongolia, Iran) consider 0.20–0.30 to match the `crude_ore` feed's CV, since those are the edges no feed ever touched. Even 0.05 alone would shrink the worst z by ~50× (5,400 → ~110) without touching the constraint code.
2. **Audit Step 5's `mask` assignment.** The current rule "everything not yet imputed is supply unless tagged use → CV=0.001" is exactly backward for the *un-imputed* universe: by construction, those edges have no data, so they deserve the *loosest* prior, not the tightest. Either invert the default (uncovered → CV=0.3) or add a `coverage`-aware branch (`covered → 0.05, uncovered → 0.30`).
3. **Cross-check coverage of `Ore | ROW` outgoing edges.** Step 1 (ParametricImputer with `ore_grade_params.csv`) fills only 820 edges out of ~854k. The `Ore | ROW` lane is almost certainly in the 460k batch that gets the flat-prior treatment — verify with `imp_log.parquet` (`imputation_step == 5` & `region == "ROW"` & `sector_from == "Ore"`).

### B — Constraint assembly: independent-noise assumption

4. **Replace `sqrt(sum(σ²))` with an effective-sample-size correction** in `SumConstraintBuilder.build_dynamic`. When supply edges share the same imputer (especially the flat prior) their errors are **perfectly correlated**, not independent. A pragmatic fix:
   ```python
   sigma_c[i] = np.sqrt(np.nansum(sigma_agg[rhs] ** 2)) + alpha * np.nansum(np.abs(sigma_agg[rhs]))
   ```
   with `alpha ∈ [0.1, 0.5]` interpolating between independent (current) and fully correlated (sum of |σ|). Or, cleanly, track the imputer ID per edge and compute a block-diagonal aggregation.
5. **Add a relative-σ floor.** Cap `σ_c[i] / |c[i]|` at e.g. 1e-2 inside `build_dynamic`. That alone would prevent the `Ore | <region>` z=3,500 fixed point and is a 2-line change.
6. **Per-row CV inflation for `ROW` and other aggregated regions.** Multiply σ_c by 3–5× when the row is tagged `region == "ROW"` to reflect the heterogeneity that aggregation hides. (Same recommendation as the previous REP; still applies.)

### C — Pipeline hygiene

7. **Make Step 5 coverage-aware**. The `agg_result` already carries `coverage` (boolean per edge). Use it inside `FlatPriorImputer` to inflate `cv` proportionally to `1 / max(coverage_neighbors, ε)` instead of using a flat value, so an isolated uncovered cell gets a wider prior than one neighboured by covered cells.
8. **Confirm the cold-start (1995) behavior.** The objective drop to −1.3e12 in 1995 is consistent with the flat-prior init zeroing out the regulariser term over most cells — but worth verifying that the 1995 solution isn't structurally unusable downstream. Re-run with `a₀(1995) =` median of `f_agg` over 1996–2010 instead of the flat prior, and compare `f*(1995)` patterns to the 2024 stable state.
9. **Re-run after (1)+(5) and re-compute this report.** Expected outcome: `sum_eq` p95 z drops from ~67 (2024) to <10; max z drops from 6,273 to <300; Ore/Mining stop dominating. If the re-run *doesn't* clean up Ore, the issue is structural in the network (Ore → SXEW/MF lanes for ROW are missing in `edge_index`, not just under-σ), and the next step is to audit `init_copper_acc.py` for those lanes.

### Priority order

1. **Fix `flat_prior_cv.csv` first** (1 file edit, no code change). This is necessary and probably sufficient to bring the report's headline numbers within the "AlmostSolved-but-meaningful" regime of the prior run.
2. **Add the σ_c relative floor** (5) (2-line patch). Defensive; protects against future imputer mis-calibration.
3. **Replace the independent-σ propagation** (4) (small refactor) — do this only after (1)+(5) clearly leave residual correlated-error problems.
