**Verdict: rejected on measurement; the incumbent faer is kept.** Seven of the eight decision items hold. The one
that fails asks whether qdldl's answers are at least as good: scored on the same QPs, its posteriors sum to Σ_t J
**3,128,188.8** against faer's **3,126,851.3** (+1,337.5, **+0.043 % vs `opt_20261019_171746_3272682b`**). That
gap is noise at the numerical floor, not a quality difference: qdldl is lower in 13 of 30 years (sign test
p ≈ 0.58), the per-year ΔJ has mean +44.6 and sd 134.9 (t ≈ 1.8), and 2021 alone carries 42 % of the sum. So
qdldl brings no quality gain to set against moving every number of the reference chain, and its speed buys
little: the period solves are 4.5× faster (233.6 → 51.8 s), but Stage 4 falls only **745 → 563 s** because the
first-year feasibility probe takes 444 s in both runs. `DIRECT_SOLVE_METHOD` stays `"faer"`.

## 1. Change

Clarabel factorises each interior-point iteration's KKT system with a sparse LDLᵀ. #70 pinned that linear solver
(faer, Clarabel's automatic choice on copper) to one thread per period. qdldl is Clarabel's other built-in
LDLᵀ, single-threaded. The scratch benchmark behind #72 found it ≈ 5× faster per period on
`const_20261012_113828_12c562c7`, with objectives moving by −122 … +311 per period in both directions. That
constraint run was pruned on 2026-10-20, so the measurement moved to the final chain, whose own faer solve is
the base; both use #70's parallel solve with 8 workers and one linear-solver thread. The probe
(`feasibility_check`) keeps Clarabel's default settings in both runs.

## 2. Decision rule and result

The eight items were fixed in the docstring of `scripts/explo/issue72_qdldl_diag.py` before any result existed.
The qdldl solve started at 08:54:27 (`logs/init_run_solver_20261020_085427.log`), the script was last edited at
08:55:31, and the run was saved at 09:02:46. Script and result share one commit, so git alone does not show
the order.

Both solves stop at Clarabel's numerical floor (ADR 0039), not at the exact optimum, so a different linear solver
lands on a different point near it. The rule therefore scores both posteriors on the same objective,
J = Σ((F − a₀)/σ_a)² + Σ_free ((G F − c)/σ_c)², recomputed from `F_opt` in the solver's conditioning (the
`roundZ_png_kg_diag._J` cross-evaluation), and adopts qdldl only if it is not worse on any item. World and
exporter retentions (Round-E (a), (c)) are pooled over 1995–2024.

## 5. Reading

- **qdldl is faster, not better.** It takes as many iterations as faer (Σ 6,915 against 6,969) and each costs
  about a fifth of the time. It stops at a different point near the floor; the per-year moves (−150 … +560 J)
  are of the size ADR 0039 already accepts as the floor's distance from the optimum, and their sum is not
  distinguishable from zero. Neither solver's answer is wrong, but nothing makes qdldl's preferable, and
  adopting it would move every number of the manuscript chain for 3 min of wall-clock.
- **The probe is the remaining lever.** At 444 of 563 s it is 79 % of Stage 4 under qdldl and 60 % under faer.
  #55's per-period probe through the thread pool is where the next minutes are.
